Dave Rauchwerk · Rauchwerk Research

Rauchwerk Research is my personal open research and concept work. Each project here is self-directed and published in the open: the code, the data, and a field report that says what works and what does not yet.

The thread running through it is checking AI against something it cannot argue with: a physics simulation, a proof checker, a fabricated circuit board, a health record that can be reset and graded. This year that meant voice agents for patients managing several long-term conditions, routing benchmarks for high-frequency boards, a SPARCstation that boots in your browser, laser-optics benches designed by agents and verified by a tracer, and seL4’s proofs ported to Lean. Before that, I co-founded Next Thing Co., maker of C.H.I.P., the $9 computer.

Use the product. Show what works. Prototype what’s next.

2026Agents · Voice · Health · Field report

Designing consumer health agents

A voice agent that answers from a patient’s health record and acts on it: refills, bookings, messages to the care team, home readings. It is tested against a digital twin of a health system instead of a real one: four synthetic patients stored as FHIR records, with a simulated clinic that answers back. One fast realtime voice acts; two slower colleague personas, a pharmacist and a nurse, take the questions that need care, each in their own voice. Anything that changes the record waits for the patient’s yes, enforced in code.

The evaluation grades the record, not the conversation: 105 graded conversations, 30 of 35 scenarios held every time, no write went through without the patient’s agreement, and the failures that remain are traced to a stage. Ten user stories, a 22-page field report and a three-minute demo with live speech both ways.

2026Benchmark · PCB · Field report

PCB Gold-Master Eval

Can machines route high-frequency boards like the engineers who shipped them? Eight fabricated, open-source Antmicro boards (PCIe, HDMI, MIPI, DDR4, DDR5) with their copper stripped, packaged as Harbor tasks and scored against the human layout on skew, length matching and impedance-correct widths. Freerouting completes up to 96% of a board and misses the high-frequency targets. Includes a 10-page field report, an arXiv paper draft and a compute-budget forecast.

2026Hardware · Work in progress

Zine Machine v0

A 40% keyboard with a Linux computer inside. ChrisChrisLoLo’s bancouver40 board, extended to carry a PocketBeagle 2 and a 4.58″ 320×960 bar display in a translucent printed case. The PCB was routed in KiCad and the case modelled through SolidCraft’s MCP server, both checked against BeagleBoard’s own 3D model. Not ready for fab yet.

2026Emulation

InfiniteSPARCjit

A SPARCstation 5 running SunOS 4.1.4 and OpenWindows in a web browser. QEMU is compiled to WebAssembly with a SPARC-to-WebAssembly JIT, and the 2 GB disk streams as a 72 MB image. The only existing wasm JIT crashed SunOS three different ways; fixing it took a headless harness and JIT bisection.

2026Agents · Optics · Hackathon

BEAMTEAM

Gemini agents design tabletop laser-optics setups for atomic and quantum physics labs, or rebuild one from a figure in a published paper, in under three minutes. A deterministic physics tracer checks every design. The output is a build kit: parts list, keyed printable baseplates and an assembly PDF.

Project pageSource & paper

2026Self-improving agents · Hackathon

SparkleTeam (RSHI)

Five open Nemotron models on Crusoe learn to load a physically simulated dishwasher. They rewrite their own prompts, tools and team structure, and keep a change only if it wins a paired, measured test. The team found real physics, and the replication caught a learned superstition: +17.8 points became −0.3.

Project pageSource & paper

2026WebGL · Horology

Assembling the 6497

The ETA 6497 hand-wound watch movement, tessellated from the manufacturer’s CAD and assembled part by part in ETA’s own order, drawn in the style of George Daniels’ Watchmaking. Wind it and it runs: the train, hands and power reserve all follow from a 3 Hz escapement.

2026Formal verification

sel4-lean

Porting the formal verification of the seL4 microkernel from Isabelle/HOL to Lean 4. The executable spec is checked against l4v’s own built Isabelle spec, and that check found real differences an earlier port had missed.

2026Agent · OpenBSD

puffy

An OpenBSD-native agent that manages, configures and programs the machine, and explains each step by pointing to the man page. It plans with a model on confidential compute; with no network it falls back to searching the OpenBSD manual and package catalog locally.

2022Manufacturing

Atari Jaguar molds, recovered

The original Atari Jaguar injection molds, brought back into service in Los Angeles to test whether US factories can still make consumer electronics. The result was transparent cases for an FPGA console, and hard lessons about volume versus value.

03 · Next Thing Co. 2013–2018

Some of the cheapest Linux computers ever sold.

Co-founder and CEO, with Gustavo Huber and Thomas Deckert, in Oakland. We started with a GIF camera and ended up making some of the cheapest Linux computers ever sold.

2016$49 handheld

PocketC.H.I.P.

A handheld Linux computer for $49: a 4.3″ touchscreen, a clicky keyboard, GPIO across the top, a five-hour battery and the PICO-8 fantasy console preinstalled. Play first, then open a terminal and start tinkering.

Tested videoMaker Faire talkReview

2016Maker Faire

Mega PocketC.H.I.P.

A giant, working PocketC.H.I.P. for Maker Faire, so a crowd could press the keys. Every key worked.

2015$9 computer

C.H.I.P.

A 1 GHz ARM computer with 512 MB of RAM, 4 GB of storage, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, for $9. The question was why the parts in a $50 Android tablet couldn’t make a $9 computer. It ran open source on mainline Linux, and the Kickstarter raised $2,071,972 from 39,560 backers, 41 times its goal.

2016–2017Modules

C.H.I.P. Pro & GR8

A $16 FCC-certified module and the $6 GR8 system-in-package, both on mainline Linux, so other companies could ship products on the platform. Engadget reported more than 100,000 C.H.I.P.s sold.

2014Camera

OTTO

A hackable GIF camera you crank: every click of the crank captures a frame. It was the first product built on the Raspberry Pi Compute Module, came out of the HAXLR8R hardware accelerator in Shenzhen, and ran code you could change in Python or Node.
