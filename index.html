2026Emulation

InfiniteSPARCjit

A SPARCstation 5 running SunOS 4.1.4 and OpenWindows in a web browser. QEMU is compiled to WebAssembly with a SPARC-to-WebAssembly JIT, and the 2 GB disk streams as a 72 MB image. The only existing wasm JIT crashed SunOS three different ways; fixing it took a headless harness and JIT bisection.

2026Agents · Optics · Hackathon

BEAMTEAM

Gemini agents design tabletop laser-optics setups for atomic and quantum physics labs, or rebuild one from a figure in a published paper, in under three minutes. A deterministic physics tracer checks every design. The output is a build kit: parts list, keyed printable baseplates and an assembly PDF.

Project pageSource & paper

2026Self-improving agents · Hackathon

SparkleTeam (RSHI)

Five open Nemotron models on Crusoe learn to load a physically simulated dishwasher. They rewrite their own prompts, tools and team structure, and keep a change only if it wins a paired, measured test. The team found real physics, and the replication caught a learned superstition: +17.8 points became −0.3.

Project pageSource & paper

2026WebGL · Horology

Assembling the 6497

The ETA 6497 hand-wound watch movement, tessellated from the manufacturer’s CAD and assembled part by part in ETA’s own order, drawn in the style of George Daniels’ Watchmaking. Wind it and it runs: the train, hands and power reserve all follow from a 3 Hz escapement.

2026Agent · OpenBSD

An OpenBSD-native agent that manages, configures and programs the machine, and explains each step by pointing to the man page. It plans with a model on confidential compute; with no network it falls back to searching the OpenBSD manual and package catalog locally.

2015$9 computer

C.H.I.P.

A 1 GHz ARM computer with 512 MB of RAM, 4 GB of storage, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, for $9. The question was why the parts in a $50 Android tablet couldn’t make a $9 computer. It ran open source on mainline Linux, and the Kickstarter raised $2,071,972 from 39,560 backers, 41 times its goal.

2016–2017Modules

C.H.I.P. Pro & GR8

A $16 FCC-certified module and the $6 GR8 system-in-package, both on mainline Linux, so other companies could ship products on the platform. Engadget reported more than 100,000 C.H.I.P.s sold.

2014Camera

OTTO

A hackable GIF camera you crank: every click of the crank captures a frame. It was the first product built on the Raspberry Pi Compute Module, came out of the HAXLR8R hardware accelerator in Shenzhen, and ran code you could change in Python or Node.
